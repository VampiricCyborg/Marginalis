### Did the EDA prediction hold?

- holdout_2025: gas-as-Other rule fired on no days (0 CISO days).
- holdout_2026ytd: gas-as-Other rule fired on CISO 2026-05-14, CISO 2026-05-15, CISO 2026-05-16, CISO 2026-05-17, CISO 2026-05-29, CISO 2026-05-30, CISO 2026-05-31, CISO 2026-06-06, CISO 2026-06-07, CISO 2026-06-19, CISO 2026-06-20, CISO 2026-06-26, CISO 2026-06-27, CISO 2026-06-28, CISO 2026-06-29 (15 CISO days).

**The frozen rule produced false positives on CISO in 2026.** On these days CISO's gas output was already very low all day (≈600–950 MWh, 3–4% of generation), and batteries reported under 'Other' discharged 6–9 GW in the evening after charging at midday. So both thresholds were crossed by legitimate storage, not by gas being relabelled (the ERCO artifact is gas *collapsing* from ~25 GW into a flat ~22 GW of 'Other'). The rule was not changed, because the method is frozen, and the results above apply it as frozen. Hold-out 1, which carries the decision, is unaffected (the rule fired on no 2025 days). A future version should key on an abrupt gas-to-Other transfer rather than on levels.

## Notes

- Hold-out strata have one observation per day (≈30 per month × hour in a single year), so each hold-out slope is noisy. The realised gap averages many of them, and its CI reflects that.
- Bootstrap replicates that leave out every week of some stratum (possible with one year of data and 4–5 weeks per month) have an undefined slope and are dropped. Their share is the `dropped reps %` column. The resampling scheme itself is the pre-registered one, unchanged.
- Only complete local days are evaluated. Split boundaries are UTC, so each split begins and ends on a partial local day, where a scheduler cannot make the full daily choice.
- 'Realised' is estimated from hold-out data, because the marginal CO₂ of load that was not actually added cannot be observed directly. Same in-BA, demand-spec limitations as the EDA (no controls; imports not counted).
